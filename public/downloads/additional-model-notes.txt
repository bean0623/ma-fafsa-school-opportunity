Additional models: definitions and interpretation
Prepared 7 October 2026

Study unit and scope

The school is the observation. The source DTA has 415 records; the unchanged
eligibility rule retains 407 with positive 2024-25 senior enrollment. This is
the eligible set in the current merged sample, not a verified census of every
Massachusetts public school enrolling seniors. Boston means the BPS district,
excluding charter schools belonging to separate charter districts.

Student low-income share is the main SES measure, entered per 10 percentage
points. The existing statewide three/four groups stay fixed across outcomes.
Campus-tract income enters separately in log base 2: its coefficient is per
doubling of the assigned tract median. It does not measure students' household
incomes or necessarily their residential neighborhoods. Its ACS vintage and
geocoding documentation remain unverified.

Statewide standard errors are clustered by district. Boston is one district,
so Boston models use heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors. The Boston
income model has two predictors, low-income share and campus income. It is
not the full statewide adjusted model. All coefficients are associations.

Outcomes and population differences

1. College destinations: four reported sector shares among college attendees.
   These are not shares of all graduates. Schools with no college attendees
   cannot contribute to destination-composition regressions. Five eligible
   schools' four sector shares sum below 100 beyond rounding tolerance. Their
   reported shares are retained, not normalized. An export identifies them;
   the reason for the shortfall is not established.

   The additional all-graduate four-year outcome multiplies the overall
   college attendance percentage by the public-plus-private four-year share,
   divided by 100. It is approximate because source percentages are rounded.
   It describes attendance by March 2026 among Class of 2025 graduates.

2. Graduate plans: ten existing category percentages plus any two/four-year
   college and four-year-college aggregates. The source fields are already
   percentages; they are not divided by graduate counts again. These are
   reported intentions of 2024-25 graduates, not observed enrollment.

   Planned minus observed college attendance is a school-level difference.
   It requires equal positive graduate counts in both reports. Matching counts
   do not establish matching individual students. The gap cannot identify
   which students changed plans or prove that FAFSA information caused a gap.

3. Disengagement: the 2025 four-year cohort dropout percentage and 2024-25
   whole-school severe chronic absence, defined by the source as missing at
   least 20% of membership days. Absence models use log total enrollment;
   other statewide models use log enrollment in grades 9-12.

4. MCAS: average scaled scores and meeting/exceeding percentages in ELA,
   mathematics and science; average growth percentiles in ELA and mathematics.
   Raw values were corroborated against official Grade 10 reports at Abby
   Kelley, Mystic Valley, Boston Latin Academy and Abington, including schools
   serving younger grades. The export omits explicit grade/group filters, and
   every school was not independently checked. Treat all eight measures as
   provisional 2025 Grade 10 academic context, not Class of 2025 outcomes.
   Science uses best Grade 9/10 performance with continuity requirements.

5. SAT: 2024-25 mathematics and reading/writing averages among test takers.
   Test-taking selection means these do not represent every senior.

6. AP: unique exam-taker count, 2024-25, all subjects/all students. The grade
   universe is not documented sufficiently to produce a verified participation
   rate. The linear count model estimates the school-size coefficient rather
   than imposing an exposure denominator or offset. Its units are students,
   not percentage points. Count-model linear predictions can be negative and
   influential large schools require review before prominent use.

7. Pathways: reported count and published percentage, 2025-26, grades 9-12
   plus special education beyond grade 12 (SP). These describe later program
   context, not Class of 2025 outcomes. Counts are not verified unique students.
   The 2024-25 school-size covariate is lagged context, not a current-year
   denominator. Missing source records remain missing, not zero.
   Published percentages above 100 are retained/flagged; an additional model
   excludes those values transparently. No percentage is capped, and the
   source does not establish whether overlap or another issue explains them.

Multiple testing and overlapping measures

Families were defined before selecting results: destinations, plans,
intention gap, disengagement, MCAS, SAT, AP and pathways. Holm corrections are
computed separately for each geography and specification, using the planned
family size. A stricter global correction uses all 33 planned added outcomes.
Unestimable constant outcomes are treated as p=1 for these planned counts.
Confidence intervals are nominal 95% intervals, not simultaneous intervals.
These additions are exploratory and have been chosen after prior project
analysis. Multiplicity corrections do not make the extension preregistered.

Compositional shares and derived aggregates overlap. Ten plans shares are not
ten independent discoveries. Dropout overlaps graduation status, and severe
absence overlaps the original chronic-absence outcome. Count coefficients,
score points and percentage-point coefficients cannot be ranked as though
they were the same outcome scale.

The original six-outcome results and their Holm adjustment are untouched.
Do not apply their p-values to the new income-adjusted or added models.

FAFSA comparisons and presenting real differences

Keep the screened May 31, 2025 count/enrolled-seniors ratio. Numerator and
denominator come from different aggregate systems, not matched students.
Do not change the denominator, impute suppression as zero, alter cutoffs,
drop schools, or choose controls because a result becomes larger.

The script reports all specified comparisons, including per-10-point low-
income associations and an interquartile contrast based on fixed percentiles
of the 407-school low-income distribution. The IQR endpoints are exported.
The IQR is a predictor contrast, not a difference between group means.

Categorical contrasts report highest-minus-lowest low-income groups statewide,
or highest-minus-middle in BPS, whose lowest statewide tercile has no schools.
Negative values mean lower FAFSA completion at the higher-low-income group.
Full-sample raw contrasts and income-complete raw contrasts are both saved.
Use the income-complete raw model to assess changes after income adjustment.

The BPS middle tercile has four usable FAFSA schools: the three exam schools
and Boston Arts Academy. Excluding exam schools leaves only Boston Arts
Academy in that group. The no-exam sensitivity is therefore not a broad
comparison of representative middle-income and high-poverty Boston schools.

Changing predictor units can make the displayed coefficient numerically
larger without changing model fit, t-statistics or evidence. A wider contrast
changes the substantive question. Report its endpoints, N and confidence
interval. A strong memo can focus on a documented gap while acknowledging
that the adjusted FAFSA relationship is uncertain and information access has
not been directly measured.
